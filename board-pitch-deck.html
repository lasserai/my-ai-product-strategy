The Case · 1 of 3

Why now

The opportunity is to move sustainability in footwear from brand-level promises to product-level evidence.
Our proposition connects three things that are usually disconnected: how a shoe is made, how it performs in real-world use, and what happens when it is returned. The immediate timing opportunity is our Q3 enterprise design-partner conversion, which gives us a commercial window to validate the proposition before committing to broader production. However, the strategy does not yet establish a specific market shift in the last 6–12 months, nor quantify competitor adoption or customer willingness to pay. We should not present market timing as proven. The six-month window is a validation opportunity, not evidence of an expiring market window.
The Case · 2 of 3

What's defensible

The moat is the physical-product learning loop (M2), not the app or the AI.
Fit Session establishes an individual baseline; the reusable sensor pod captures real-world wear; structured return codes connect actual product failures to formulation batches; laboratory degradation tests validate what happens to the materials; and returned-pair data informs the next formulation and the next pair. This creates a relationship between runner, physical shoe and material performance that Strava or Garmin cannot reproduce simply by adding another shoe-mileage reminder. We are explicitly weak on the preference loop (2/5): those platforms already own training histories and gear mileage. Our defence is to own the fit session, physical wear evidence and tuned replacement product rather than compete for another dashboard. The moat is not established yet. It must be earned through wear-data pipeline linked to shoe ID and formulation batch, structured return codes, lab degradation testing linked to returned pairs, and Gen-2 tuned pair from returned-pair data. Six months of execution should establish whether this data advantage is real.
The Case · 3 of 3

The economics

50% current gross margin falls to approximately 35% under the proposed retention and circular-service model; the current AI-adjusted gross-margin estimate is only 7%.
That gap is the commercial problem we must resolve before scaling. The proposed pricing is €210 for the first shoe-and-pod bundle, €180 for repeat shoes, and €39/year for App+. The basic app includes a €30 return credit, issued only against a physical return and spent on the next pair. This makes the incentive outcome-based rather than an unconditional discount. Inference costs are controlled through a 95% rules-first / small-and-mid-model routing target, with frontier inference reserved for approximately 5% of requests. App+ usage is expected to cost more because of deeper training summaries. The intended margin recovery comes from returns, repair, retention and repeat purchases, not cheaper inference alone. But the 35% net-margin target, actual AI COGS per runner, reverse-logistics cost and break-even repeat-purchase rate are not validated. 10x human-review workload and AI cost testing, verified AI COGS per unit, the gross-to-net margin bridge, and break-even volume and repeat-purchase rate must establish those numbers before we claim a scalable business model.
The Risks

What could break this

Trust / Failure
The biggest reputational risk is a publicly shared Impact Record containing a false sustainability claim. A certification could be invalid, a material batch could fail independent testing, a durability prediction could be wrong, or a compostability claim could be contradicted by actual recovery performance. That turns our strongest proposed differentiator into a greenwashing liability for both the company and the runner sharing the record. Our reliability contract requires at least 95% of golden-dataset cases to pass and 100% of sustainability-claim and safety cases to pass. Confidence is determined by source quality, rule checks and topic risk, not simply by the model's own confidence score. Conflicting evidence, missing sources and high-risk claims go to human review. Certification provenance, independent durability and recovery testing, a 150-case golden dataset and release gates are prerequisites. The public Impact Record stays blocked until the evidence chain and legal approval are complete. The current 22-case dataset is a starting point, not proof of production reliability. We also have an unresolved evaluation gap: the partner-identified failure mode is not documented in the strategy. That must be closed before the board treats the reliability assessment as complete.
Scale / Governance
At 10x usage, two things can break the business: inference and human-review costs can consume contribution margin, while weak evidence and model drift can allow incorrect product or sustainability advice to reach customers. 10x human-review workload and AI cost testing explicitly tests the 10x cost and review workload; verified AI COGS per unit measures actual AI COGS; the gross-to-net margin bridge reconciles gross and net margin. The operating design uses rules first, a small classifier for routine routing and a replaceable frontier provider for genuinely difficult requests. We will not ship autonomous agents in this version. Models recommend; named people approve and execute formulation changes, refunds, exchanges, supplier changes and external data sharing. A formulation recommendation within 10% of the compostability floor requires sustainability-lead approval. Monthly reviews cover accuracy, drift, guardrail metrics and failures. Vendor portability is ready, but operational resilience and the economics of human review still need validation. No scale-up decision should precede that evidence.
Competitive
The single scenario that forces a kill is Strava or Garmin launching a credible shoe-personalisation and replacement proposition that matches our user value while we fail to demonstrate that physical-product data produces a measurable advantage in fit, durability or repeat purchase. We cannot win an app-level feature race against platforms that already own runner relationships. Our defence is a better physical product informed by the runner's actual returned pair. The kill test is the controlled 300–500-runner pilot against a control group without the reward, with numerical thresholds agreed before enrolment. If treatment does not outperform control on the pre-registered primary outcome by six months, or breaches a critical safety threshold, stop or redesign the bet. More specifically, if fit personalisation fails to improve the agreed fit score after sufficient 50 km observations, stop expanding that investment. If returned-pair recommendations fail to outperform the repair team's baseline after 100 assessed pairs, stop model development. We fund evidence of product advantage, not feature parity.
